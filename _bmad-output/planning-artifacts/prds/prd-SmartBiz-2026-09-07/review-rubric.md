# PRD Quality Review — SmartBiz BD

**PRD:** `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/prds/prd-SmartBiz-2026-09-07/prd.md` (1188 lines, FR-1–FR-68, NFR-1–NFR-21, SM-1–SM-9, SM-C1–SM-C4)
**Rubric:** `.claude/skills/bmad-prd/assets/prd-validation-checklist.md`
**Upstream read:** `briefs/brief-SmartBiz-2026-09-06/brief.md`, `addendum.md`

## Overall verdict

This is a well-argued PRD with a real thesis, honest trade-off accounting, and the best counter-metric set I have seen in a capstone document — and it has one hole directly through the middle of its flagship journey. **UJ-3, the journey the entire "profit truth" positioning rests on, is not implementable by any requirement in the document**: Rehana fills in missing Purchase Cost from the Completeness Indicator drill-down, but no FR grants the ability to write Purchase Cost onto an existing Order Line, FR-16 forbids Order Line edits from Packed onward, and both FR-8 and the §3 glossary state Purchase Cost is a property of received units recorded at Stock-In and "never inferred, never defaulted." The loop that makes the completeness indicator *actionable* — the single feature that distinguishes this product from a spreadsheet — has no requirement behind it.

Second structural problem: the §4 cap mechanism, which the document nominates as its answer to the accepted "not achievable at this depth" finding, does not cover §5.14 (audit and reversal) or §7 (localization and Bangla rendering) at all. Those two are among the three most expensive things in the build, and one of them — reversibility limited to an enumerated operation set — is *one of the three named reductions that paid for the variant matrix*. The reduction exists as a bullet inside FR-64, not as a row in the table whose "does not include at this tier" column §8 makes binding on story acceptance criteria. The capping mechanism is missing the thing it was built to hold.

What holds up: decision-readiness is genuinely strong, non-goals do real work, the Recognition Basis / Completeness Indicator concept is a legitimate product idea rather than framing, and the counter-metrics (SM-C1–C4) name the exact ways a developer under deadline pressure would cheat. What is at risk: done-ness (nine reports share two FRs and zero field definitions), internal consistency of the settlement state machine (FR-30 vs FR-34), and §6.2, which contradicts itself in two adjacent rows.

## Commissioned question: are the §4 caps load-bearing, or decorative?

**Verdict: enforceable in form, not load-bearing in effect.** They will stop scope creep and will not stop the deadline.

Three things are genuinely right about the mechanism. The "does not include at this tier" entries are concrete and checkable, not adjectival — "more than three Variant Dimensions," "PDF for the other six reports," "per-variant images" are things you can point at in a pull request. §8 binds story acceptance criteria to them explicitly. SM-8 makes cap conformance a graded metric. That is more than most tiering sections achieve.

But the mechanism does not reduce the build, for four reasons:

1. **The exclusions are mostly strawmen.** Walk the "does not include" columns and ask which entries remove work that was actually going to be attempted in three weeks: SSO, org hierarchies, subscription billing, barcodes, product import, receipt OCR, budgets, approval workflows, multi-warehouse, stock transfers, batch/lot/expiry, cycle counts, purchase orders, dunning, interest, credit limits, loyalty schemes, label printing, pickup scheduling, courier rate cards, forecasting. None of these was on the critical path. Perhaps five entries across the whole table represent real deferred work (PDF for six reports, supplier payments, partial shipment, offline sync, per-variant images). The table reads as a ceiling but functions mostly as a list of things nobody proposed.

2. **The "must reach" column is the binding half, and it is bigger than the FR set.** The cap table is written as a limit but its left column is a floor, and in three cases the floor exceeds what §5 specifies. The Reports row commits to "all nine reports … with date filtering, CSV export for all nine, and Bangla PDF export for three" against FR-61's single bullet "displays totals appropriate to its subject." The Customer due row commits to "due collection status," which appears in no FR. The Supplier row commits to "outstanding balance," which FR-60 says is "recorded" with no source, no decrement path, and no owning requirement. §4 therefore *adds* obligations rather than bounding them.

3. **The two most expensive cross-cutting capabilities have no tier.** Audit + reversal across six operation types with a per-type invariant test, and a Bangla shaping pipeline serving PDF plus canvas image rendering, are uncapped. So is FR-6 draft persistence, FR-66's two-locale i18n with an untranslated-string test, and FR-67's bidirectional Bengali numeral handling. The cap table covers twelve module rows and the build is not made of twelve modules.

4. **§4.4 has no trigger and no leverage.** "If delivery pressure forces reduction" names no date, no signal, and no decider. A retreat ladder without a trip point gets descended at the deadline, which is the same as not existing. And the ladder's four rungs are cheap: dropping a variant dimension, cutting AI insights, cutting reports, simplifying the risk ratio — call it three or four days of savings against an overrun the upstream review measured in weeks. Meanwhile §4.4 declares the three Hero modules untouchable "under any circumstance," which removes the only lever with real mass. The cheap, high-yield cuts that are *not* on the ladder: the English locale (FR-66 doubles all copy), Bengali numeral input (FR-67), local drafts (FR-6), the Platform Administrator surface (FR-46 — an entire second auth context for a stakeholder who appears once), the Daily Summary image path (FR-40, distinct from the PDF path in every way that costs time), and per-Order unit economics (FR-18).

**What would make the caps load-bearing:** add cap rows for audit/reversal and localization; add a dated checkpoint to §4.4 ("if the settlement import is not matching rows by end of week 2, rung 1 fires"); and add three rungs above the current rung 1 drawn from the cheap-cut list. Without those, §4 is a good-faith control that will be discovered to be non-binding at the point it was supposed to bind.

## Decision-readiness — strong

Trade-offs are named with what was surrendered, not just what was chosen. §4's preamble does the unusual thing of stating that an independent review judged the scope unachievable and that the judgement "was accepted rather than argued away" — then §5.8 restates the variant matrix as "the project's largest schedule risk" rather than quietly normalising it. §10.2 flags the Meta integration omission as "the most competitively significant omission … a known weakness rather than a virtue." The six `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts sit at genuine tensions (FR-2 refusing an untestable accuracy target, FR-34 admitting the settlement simplification "is not elegant, which is accepted," FR-58 on double-counting, FR-68 on the Bangla late-stage blocker) rather than at safe checkpoints. All eight Open Questions are actually open and seven carry an owner.

Where it falls short is the decision the document does not make: **there is no build sequence.** For a solo developer with 15–25 working days, the highest-value planning artifact is an ordered list of what gets built in week 1 and what the go/no-go looks like at the end of it. §4.4 gestures at retreat but never at order of construction. FR-68's note ("should be proven with a spike early") is the only sequencing statement in 1188 lines, and it is advice rather than a commitment.

### Findings

- **high** No build sequence or dated checkpoint (§4.4, §10.1) — the document decides depth per module but never order, so nothing tells the developer which of the 68 FRs are week-1 obligations. §4.4's retreat trigger is the unfalsifiable "if delivery pressure forces reduction." *Fix:* add §4.5 with a three-phase order (thread spine + tenancy → settlement + profit → breadth) and a hard mid-point date at which rung 1 of §4.4 fires automatically unless the Hero flows demo end-to-end.
- **medium** Retreat ladder omits every cheap cut (§4.4) — the four rungs are low-yield while FR-66's English locale, FR-67's Bengali numeral input, FR-6's drafts, FR-46's Platform Administrator, and FR-40's image renderer are all higher-savings and lower-risk. *Fix:* insert them as rungs 0a–0e above the current rung 1.
- **low** Upstream risk ranking silently reversed (§5.8 vs `brief.md:163`) — the brief calls the variant matrix the "second-largest schedule risk" behind timeline-versus-breadth; the PRD promotes it to "the project's largest." §0 gives the PRD precedence so this is legal, but an unremarked reversal of an upstream risk ranking is worth a sentence. *Fix:* note the reclassification, or restore the brief's ordering.

## Substance over theater — strong

One persona, named, with volumes attributed and an assumption tag in §13 admitting she is drawn from the brief rather than from research. Six user journeys that each drive at least one requirement. The differentiation claims are checkable rather than rhetorical — FR-18's "the capability no surveyed competitor offers" and §5.4's "the mechanism no surveyed competitor offers" both trace to the addendum's competitor matrix, and §7 does the honest opposite by explicitly demoting Bangla UI to "a baseline requirement, not a differentiator — nearly every competitor offers a Bangla interface." That last move is the strongest single signal in the document that the analysis is real.

The NFRs are mostly sized rather than copied: NFR-10 states the sizing basis (50 orders/day, five concurrent members, "not against invented scale"), and NFR-11–14 give per-path numbers. Three NFRs slip back into adjectives.

The counter-metrics deserve specific credit. SM-C1 and SM-C2 name the two ways this build will actually be corrupted — loosening parser extraction and loosening match criteria to make demo numbers look better — and SM-C2's formulation ("An unmatched row costs the user a moment; a wrong match costs them the truth") is the thesis stated as an engineering constraint. This is not theater.

### Findings

- **medium** Three NFRs are adjectives, not bounds (NFR-16, NFR-21, FR-67) — "minimum touch size suitable for one-thumb phone use" names no pixel value; "a cold start … within a period that keeps the demonstration usable" is unfalsifiable; "dates render in a format unambiguous to the user" names no format. NFR-17 gets this right by citing WCAG 2.1 AA. *Fix:* 44 px; "within 15 seconds, with a skeleton state from 500 ms"; name the format (e.g. `DD MMM YYYY`) and require it identically across screen, CSV, and PDF.
- **low** FR-22's simulated courier is partly decorative (FR-22, §4.1) — "advances through status transitions on demand" duplicates FR-11's transition model with no added demonstration value, and the interface-parity bullet is the only part that earns the adapter argument. *Fix:* keep the adapter seam and the labelling; cut the on-demand status advancement, which FR-11 already provides.

## Strategic coherence — adequate

There is a real thesis and the features do serve it: FR-37 and FR-38 are the thesis stated as requirements, FR-8's historical-cost capture and FR-56's movement-sum invariant exist to make those two trustworthy, and §5.4's "no discrepancy is ever silently absorbed" is the same commitment applied to cash. §11's decision to make metrics demonstration-and-test-verifiable rather than adoption-inferred is correct for the context and stated as a reasoned choice. SM-1–SM-5 each cite the FRs they validate.

Coherence breaks at two points. First, the headline arithmetic claim is wrong. Second, the profit vocabulary the thesis depends on is under-defined at exactly the point where Expenses enter, which means the product's central number has no single agreed definition in the document that defines it.

### Findings

- **critical** The identity the positioning rests on is false (FR-23 bullet 4, §3 "Money With Couriers") — FR-23 asserts Money With Couriers "equals Earned Profit minus Realized Profit in its cash component," and the glossary calls it "the difference between the two Recognition Bases." It is not. Money With Couriers is a sum of **COD Amount** — gross cash — on delivered-unsettled Orders. Earned minus Realized Profit on those same Orders is revenue *minus Purchase Cost minus Courier Charge*. The two differ by exactly the cost of goods and the courier fee, which is to say by the whole thing the product exists to track. "In its cash component" is doing load-bearing hand-waving. This is stated as a testable consequence and it will fail the test. *Fix:* delete the identity claim from both places, or restate it correctly: *Earned Profit − Realized Profit = the margin embedded in Money With Couriers*, and Money With Couriers is the gross cash figure. Then decide which one the dashboard shows as "the difference between the bases," because they are different numbers.
- **high** Gross and net profit are undefined, and Expense allocation across Recognition Bases is undefined (§3 vs FR-36, FR-58, FR-61) — the glossary defines exactly two profit terms, both of them gross (`revenue − Purchase Cost − Courier Charge`). FR-36 then displays "gross profit and net profit," FR-58 defines net as gross minus Expenses "on both Recognition Bases," and FR-61 promises a "gross-to-net chain." So the product actually has four profit figures and names two. Worse, Realized Profit is defined as "the same computation restricted to settled Orders," but Expenses are not settled or unsettled — there is no defined rule for which Expenses belong to a realized-basis period. The architect will invent one, and the number the thesis rests on will be whatever they invent. *Fix:* add four glossary entries (Earned Gross, Earned Net, Realized Gross, Realized Net) or two plus an explicit modifier convention; and state the Expense allocation rule for the realized basis (Expenses are cash-dated, so realized net = realized gross − Expenses dated in period, with the basis mismatch declared on the surface).
- **high** Costs recorded on Returned and Failed Delivery Orders reach no profit figure (FR-13 bullet 3, FR-15 bullet 3 vs §3 Earned Profit) — both FRs require the Courier Charge to "remain recorded as a cost, because it was genuinely paid," which is exactly right as a principle. But the glossary confines Earned Profit to "Delivered Orders," and a Returned or Failed Delivery Order is not Delivered. The charge is recorded and then lands nowhere. Both FRs also say the Order "contributes no revenue and no Earned Profit," when the honest statement is that it contributes *negative* profit equal to the charge. *Fix:* extend the Earned/Realized Profit definitions to `revenue − Purchase Cost − Courier Charge across Delivered, Returned, and Failed Delivery Orders`, and change the two bullets to say the contribution is negative rather than zero.
- **medium** SM-6 smuggles back the parser accuracy target FR-2 and SM-C1 refuse (SM-6 vs FR-2 Notes, SM-C1) — FR-2's note argues at length that no numeric accuracy target is testable without a labelled corpus, and SM-C1 instructs the developer *not* to optimise extraction breadth. SM-6 then requires "no more than one manual field correction" per pasted order, which is an accuracy target with a threshold of one, measured against a scripted demonstration the developer also authors. *Fix:* restate SM-6 as the behavioural contract it claims to be — one paste, one confirm, all extracted values visibly marked and editable — and drop the correction count, or move it to §12 alongside Open Question 1.
- **medium** SM-1 is falsified by FR-14 (SM-1 vs FR-14 bullet 5) — SM-1 requires "every Order reaches a terminal position with no state in the order-to-cash chain unaccounted for," while FR-14 deliberately allows returned goods to "remain unreceived indefinitely … the system does not assume it will arrive." That is the correct product behaviour and it means a legitimate Order can sit non-terminal forever. *Fix:* restate SM-1 as mutual consistency of status / stock effect / settlement position / profit contribution — which is what its own measurement clause already says — and drop "reaches a terminal position."
- **low** Damage write-off never reaches profit (FR-55 bullet 3) — written-off stock is valued at Purchase Cost and "reported as a loss in the Inventory Report" only. A profit-truth product that excludes shrinkage from the Profit Summary is making a substantive accounting choice silently. *Fix:* either route it into net profit as a cost line, or add a `[NOTE FOR PM]` saying it is deliberately excluded and why.

## Done-ness clarity — thin

Most FRs carry genuinely testable consequences, and several are exemplary: FR-11's transition enumeration, FR-44's cross-tenant not-found requirement, FR-56's summation invariant, FR-64's per-type reversal assertion, FR-68's known-difficult-conjunct fixture. The `Out of scope` blocks under individual FRs do real closing work.

But three areas are not implementable as written, and one of them is 25% of the Full tier.

### Findings

- **critical** UJ-3's climax has no enabling requirement and is blocked by three others (FR-38 bullet 3 vs FR-8, FR-16, §3 Purchase Cost) — FR-38 requires that the Completeness Indicator be actionable: selecting it navigates to the Order Lines missing Purchase Cost, and "filling missing costs and returning updates both the figure and the indicator." UJ-3 shows Rehana doing exactly this for nine items. Nothing in the document permits writing Purchase Cost onto an existing Order Line. FR-8 says the Order Line's cost is "derived from the Stock Level's recorded cost" at allocation and that a later cost change "does not alter the Purchase Cost stored on an existing Order Line." FR-16 says that from Packed onward "Order Lines and their quantities are not editable" — and the Orders that need backfilling are Delivered, because only Delivered Orders carry profit. The glossary reinforces it: Purchase Cost is "recorded at Stock-In … Not a property of a Product, but of the units received. Never inferred, never defaulted." FR-4 bullet 3 then manufactures the exact population that can never be completed — inline-created Products with no Stock-In, whose Order Lines "count against the Completeness Indicator." So the product will show incompleteness it offers no way to resolve, which inverts the thesis: the indicator becomes a permanent accusation rather than a call to action. *Fix:* add an FR — call it FR-69 — for retrospective Purchase Cost capture: an Order Line lacking Purchase Cost may have one supplied directly by a Role with cost visibility; the write creates an Audit Entry per FR-63 with before/after; it is exempted from FR-16's Packed-onward freeze and named as such in FR-16; it is added to FR-64's reversible set or explicitly excluded; and the glossary's Purchase Cost entry gains a sentence permitting late capture while still forbidding inference and defaults. Also decide whether supplying it here back-fills the Stock Level's cost or only the Order Line, because those give different answers on the next sale.
- **critical** Nine reports, two FRs, zero field definitions (FR-61, FR-62 vs §4.2 Reports row) — FR-61's operative bullet is "each accepts a date range and displays totals appropriate to its subject." That is the whole specification for Sales, Order, Product Sales, Inventory, Expense, Customer Due, and Supplier Due. Only Profit Summary and COD Settlement get content bullets. Story creation cannot produce acceptance criteria from this, CSV export (FR-62: "the same figures the on-screen report displays") has no defined column set, and SM-8's cap conformance walk has nothing to walk. The Reports module is a Full-tier commitment specified at Thin-tier resolution. *Fix:* add a table with one row per report giving its rows-are dimension, its column set, and its totals — nine rows, maybe forty lines of document, and it is the single highest-leverage addition available.
- **critical** FR-64's dependency check requires lot tracking that §4.2 forbids (FR-64 bullet 5 vs §4.2 Inventory "does not include … batch, lot, or expiry tracking") — the bullet requires refusing reversal of "a Stock-In whose units are already consumed by a Delivered Order." Knowing *which* Stock-In's units a given Order Line consumed requires per-lot consumption tracing. The Inventory cap explicitly excludes batch and lot tracking, and §12 Q3 leaves the cost-flow method open, so under weighted-average costing the information does not exist even in principle. This is a requirement that cannot be built inside its own module's cap, and it is stated as testable. *Fix:* replace with a cap-compatible check — refuse a Stock-In reversal that would drive the Stock Level below zero, or below the quantity already allocated to non-Cancelled Orders — and say plainly that reversal is quantity-safe rather than lot-accurate.
- **high** The Completeness Indicator measures one of three incompleteness sources (FR-38, §8 "Safety of financial data") — §8 states the honesty commitment absolutely: "No profit figure is ever displayed without a Recognition Basis and a Completeness Indicator." But FR-38 defines the indicator solely over Order Lines lacking Purchase Cost. A Realized Profit figure computed with **expected** rather than actual Courier Charge (FR-24 permits exactly this until settlement), or standing on an Order with an open **Disputed** variance (FR-31 keeps it visible but does not exclude it from the figure or flag the figure), or over a period whose Expenses are simply not yet entered, is materially incomplete and displays as complete. FR-38 bullet 4 makes this worse by requiring the figure be "shown as complete" when Purchase Cost is present — so the product will actively assert completeness it has not verified. This is the one place where the honesty commitment is asserted rather than enforced, and it is the commitment the whole document is built on. *Fix:* redefine the Completeness Indicator over three named components — cost completeness (N of M Order Lines), charge completeness (N of M Orders with actual rather than expected Courier Charge), and open variance exposure (৳X in Disputed variances affecting this figure) — and require all three on any realized-basis figure. Add "Expenses entered through {date}" to the Profit Summary. Then update §3's Completeness Indicator entry, which currently locks the form to "based on N of M items."
- **high** Automatic matching cannot match the case UJ-2 exists to demonstrate (FR-28 bullet 1) — matching falls back to "Customer phone number combined with amount" with no tolerance defined. A short-paid row's amount does not equal the Order's COD Amount by definition, so if the amount comparison is exact, the phone+amount rule can never match a short payment; if it is inexact, no bound is given and SM-C2's warning about wrong matches has no threshold to respect. UJ-2's ৳150 short payment is precisely this row. *Fix:* state the tolerance and its direction explicitly (e.g. phone match plus amount within the lower of ৳200 or 5%, matched rows over tolerance left unmatched), and note that a matched-with-variance row is the intended outcome.
- **high** Settlement state machine contradicts itself on partial payment (FR-30 bullet 1 vs FR-34 bullets 1 and 3) — FR-30 says a Settlement Match "sets the Order's COD collection status to settled." FR-34 says a Courier paying less than the full COD Amount "produces a Settlement Variance per FR-31 and **does not partially settle the Order**." But a variance can only arise from a match (FR-31 bullet 1 compares "the amount received" to the Order's COD Amount), so the same event both settles and does not settle the Order. FR-31 bullet 5's "An Accepted variance adjusts Realized Profit" then has no defined subject: adjusts which Order's contribution, by how much, on which date. And FR-34 bullet 3 says a second Batch paying the same Order produces "a variance" on an Order already settled, with no rule for what that variance is measured against. *Fix:* decide that a match always settles and the variance is a settled-with-shortfall adjustment; state that Realized Profit for a settled Order uses the actual received amount and actual Courier Charge; and define the second-payment case as an Accepted variance on the original Order reducing the shortfall.
- **high** "Rejecting the row" is a fourth disposition that cannot exist (FR-34 bullet 2 vs §3 Disposition, FR-29 bullet 4, FR-32 bullet 3) — FR-34 offers resolving a multi-Order row "by rejecting the row for manual splitting outside the system." The glossary enumerates exactly three Dispositions (Accepted, Disputed, Written Off), and both FR-29 and FR-32 forbid closing a Batch while any row is "neither matched nor dispositioned." A rejected row is neither, so it permanently blocks batch close. *Fix:* either add Excluded as a fourth Disposition to the glossary and to FR-29/FR-31/FR-32, or delete the rejection option and require Written Off with a note.
- **medium** Four consequences labelled testable are not (FR-17, FR-2, FR-40, FR-48) — FR-17: "identifies at a glance which Orders need attention." FR-2: "matches more than one Product with equal confidence," where the parser has no confidence model defined anywhere. FR-40: "formatted to remain legible when pasted into a messaging application." FR-48: the Risk Ratio's formula is the requirement and is never given — "computed from the Customer's return rate and Due history using a formula displayed to the user" defers the substance to the implementer, and §4.4's retreat rung ("drops to return-rate only") implies a formula that was decided somewhere and not written down. *Fix:* FR-17 — name the three attention badges as required list columns (they are already named in the same bullet). FR-2 — replace "equal confidence" with "more than one Product whose name contains the matched token." FR-40 — specify plain text, no tabs, under N characters, line-broken at 40 columns. FR-48 — write the formula.
- **medium** FR-37 and SM-2 assert testability against an enumeration that does not exist — both depend on "enumerating every profit-bearing surface," and the document never enumerates them. Without the list, the product's headline correctness metric is unmeasurable. *Fix:* enumerate them in FR-37 (dashboard headline, FR-18 per-Order economics, Profit Summary, Sales Report, Product Sales Report, COD Settlement Report, Daily Summary text, Daily Summary image, all three PDFs, all nine CSVs, each Insight Query answer) and have the test iterate that list.
- **medium** FR-61's Role gate rests on an undefined classification — "a report containing figures a Role may not see is not merely emptied but unavailable" requires knowing which of the nine are money-bearing. §6.2 has rows for "Run money-bearing reports" and "Run Inventory Report" and does not say which reports are which. Is Product Sales money-bearing? It shows revenue, possibly margin. *Fix:* add a money-bearing / not column to the nine-report table.

## Scope honesty — strong

This is the document's best dimension. §9's nine non-goals are each stated as an identity claim ("SmartBiz is not a courier") rather than a feature omission, which is the right shape. §10.2 gives a reason per exclusion and marks two as paid-for reductions. Per-FR `Out of scope` blocks close off eleven specific over-reads. §4's preamble concedes the adverse review finding in its first sentence. §13 surfaces thirteen assumptions and marks the inherited ones, including the genuinely uncomfortable one — "Sellers are willing to copy a whole message and paste it … *contradicted by competitor expectations*."

Open-items density is appropriate: eight Open Questions and six `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts against a green-light-to-build PRD is on the high side but every one names a real fork, and seven of eight questions carry an owner and a deadline expressed as a build milestone. Q5 self-identifies as "the highest-risk open question in the document," which is correct and is the kind of thing PRDs usually bury.

### Findings

- **high** §4 does not cap audit/reversal (§5.14) or localization (§7) — the cap table has twelve module rows; §5 has fifteen feature subsections plus §7. FR-63, FR-64, FR-66, FR-67, and FR-68 are bounded by nothing, and §0's claim that "§4 sets a depth ceiling for each of the twelve modules … so every requirement in §5 is read as bounded by its module's cap" is therefore false for five requirements. Most pointedly: "reversibility limited to an enumerated operation set" is one of the three reductions that paid for the variant matrix, and it lives as a bullet inside FR-64 rather than as a row in the table §8 makes binding on story acceptance criteria. The commitment that was traded away is not in the ledger. *Fix:* add two rows — Audit and reversal (Full; must reach: Audit Entry recording across the ten enumerated change types, reversal across the six enumerated operations; does not include: reversal of master-data edits, bulk or cascading undo, reversal of a reversal, lot-accurate dependency tracing) and Localization and Bangla rendering (Full; must reach: Bangla-first UI, taka formatting, shared shaping pipeline for three PDFs and the summary image; does not include: right-to-left support, additional locales, Bengali numeral input, per-report typography).
- **medium** Assumptions Index does not round-trip (§13) — the index cites FR-1, FR-5, FR-8, FR-21, FR-25, FR-28, FR-48, FR-51, NFR-10, NFR-21, but there are zero inline `[ASSUMPTION: …]` tags in the document (verified: 0 occurrences). A reader arriving at FR-21 — "An Order reaching Delivered sets COD collection status to collected by courier by default" — has no signal that this rests on an unvalidated inference about courier behaviour. The index works as a checklist and not as a warning system. *Fix:* add inline tags at each of the ten cited locations.
- **medium** FR-8 cites a decision the addendum does not contain (FR-8 Out of scope vs §12 Q3) — FR-8 states the cost-flow method "is an architecture decision recorded in `addendum.md`." I grepped the addendum: no mention of FIFO, weighted average, last cost, or cost flow anywhere. §12 Q3 correctly lists the choice as open and owned by the architecture step. FR-8's cross-reference is to a decision that has not been made. *Fix:* point FR-8 at §12 Q3 instead of the addendum.

## Downstream usability — thin

The FR numbering is clean — FR-1 through FR-68 contiguous, no gaps, no duplicates, verified. Every internal cross-reference I chased resolves (FR-9, FR-14, FR-16, FR-22, FR-23, FR-27, FR-29, FR-30, FR-31, FR-36, FR-37, FR-38, FR-43, FR-44, FR-45, FR-46, FR-52, FR-53, FR-56, FR-58, FR-60, FR-62, FR-63, FR-64, FR-65, FR-68, NFR-7, NFR-10, NFR-15, NFR-20, NFR-21, §5.2, §6.2, §7, §12). Sections stand alone reasonably well. All six UJs have a named protagonist. §11's SM-to-FR mapping gives story creation a traceability spine most PRDs lack.

The problem is what UX and architecture would have to invent, and the answer is: quite a lot, and some of it is product-level rather than design-level.

### Findings

- **high** §6.2 grants a capability no FR defines (§6.2 row "Manage Members and Roles") — Owner can manage Members and Roles. Nothing in §5.6 specifies listing Members, changing a Member's Role, or removing a Member, and nothing says what happens to a removed Member's Audit Entries and authored Orders. FR-43 covers only invitation and redemption. Epic generation will produce a Member management story with no requirement to cite. *Fix:* add an FR for Member listing, Role change (with an Audit Entry), and Member deactivation with history preserved.
- **high** No user journey exists for two of the five Roles (§2.3 vs §6.2) — all six UJs are Rehana the Owner; Sumi appears as an object in UJ-4, not a protagonist. §6.2 gives Accountant and Inventory Staff materially distinct product surfaces — Accountant can see cost and profit and reverse operations but cannot touch stock or status; Inventory Staff sees Purchase Cost but no margin and can record return receipts. FR-39 requires their dashboards be genuinely different compositions, "absent, not masked." UX will design two of five roles from a permission matrix with no journey, no volume, and no scenario. Note also that UJ-5's promise — the JTBD "undo a mistake without fear" — is unavailable to Inventory Staff, the role that makes stock mistakes, because §6.2 restricts reversal to Owner and Accountant. *Fix:* add UJ-7 (Inventory Staff receives a shipment and corrects an error) and UJ-8 (Accountant closes the month and inspects a variance), and reconcile UJ-5's promise with who can actually keep it.
- **high** §6.2 contradicts itself in two adjacent rows (§6.2 rows 2–3) — "Change Order Status: Sales Staff Yes" and "Cancel Order / record Return: Sales Staff No." Per FR-11, Cancelled and Returned *are* Order Statuses. The matrix simultaneously grants and denies Sales Staff the same operation, and there is no row at all for setting Failed Delivery. *Fix:* replace row 2 with an explicit per-transition grid — Sales Staff may take New→Confirmed→Packed→Shipped→Delivered and no terminal-negative transition — and add Failed Delivery.
- **high** FR-64 lets the Accountant perform, via reversal, three operations §6.2 forbids them (FR-64 bullet 1 vs §6.2) — the reversible set includes Stock-In, Adjustment, and Order Status transition. §6.2 gives Accountant `No` on Record Stock-In, `No` on Adjust stock, and `No` on Change Order Status, while granting them Reverse an operation. So an Accountant can write inventory movements and change Order Status through the reversal path while being barred from the forward path. Conversely Manager, who can do all three, cannot undo any of them. §6.2's stated rationale — "the person who can change financial records is not the same person who reviews them" — is sound for *reading* the audit trail and incoherent for *reversal*, which is a write with real stock and status effects. *Fix:* split the matrix row into "View Audit Entries" (Owner, Accountant) and "Reverse an operation" (Owner, Manager for operations within their forward permissions; Accountant for Payment, Settlement Match, and Expense only). Then update FR-64 to state that reversal requires permission for the forward operation.
- **medium** FR-16 and FR-20 disagree on courier reassignment (FR-16 bullet 2 vs FR-20 bullet 3) — FR-16 says that from Packed onward "status, Courier assignment, and settlement fields remain editable," which includes Delivered. FR-20 says an Order "may be reassigned to a different Courier while not yet Shipped." §4.1's cap adds a third boundary: no editing after Delivered "beyond status and settlement fields," which excludes Courier. Three statements, three boundaries. *Fix:* pick one — reassignment permitted through Packed, Courier frozen at Shipped — and align all three.
- **medium** FR-7 gives a Variant a selling price, which the cap and glossary forbid (FR-7 bullet 1 vs §4.2 Product row, §3 Variant) — FR-7 says "the unit selling price defaults from the Product **or Variant**." §4.2 excludes "variant-level pricing overrides" at this tier, and the glossary's Variant entry lists SKU, Stock Level, and Purchase Cost and no price. Three words in FR-7 create a per-variant price column, a matrix-wide pricing UI, and a cap breach. *Fix:* "defaults from the Product" — one price per Product, editable per Order Line, which FR-7 already permits.
- **medium** FR-12 counts cancellations into a ratio whose formula excludes them (FR-12 bullet 3 vs FR-48 bullet 1, §3 Customer Risk Ratio) — FR-12 says a Cancelled Order "counts toward the Customer Risk Ratio." FR-48 and the glossary both define the ratio over return rate and Due history only. *Fix:* add cancellation rate as a third named input to FR-48 and to the glossary, or drop the claim from FR-12.
- **medium** Return To Stock has no cost basis, so the cost-flow method cannot be applied (FR-14 bullets 3–4 vs §12 Q3) — a Return To Stock movement increases the Stock Level, and neither FR-14 nor the Inventory Movement glossary entry requires it to carry a Purchase Cost. Under any cost-flow method, restocking units at an unknown cost corrupts the cost of the next sale, which propagates straight into Realized Profit. The architect must invent this. *Fix:* require the Return To Stock movement to carry the Purchase Cost recorded on the originating Order Line, and say so in FR-14 and in the glossary.
- **medium** Architecture must invent at least six things beyond the eight logged in §12 — the amount tolerance in FR-28; the per-Business Order identifier sequence (FR-10 requires unique, sequential, never reused, on free-tier infrastructure NFR-20 says has no always-warm process); the Supplier balance derivation (FR-60); the Expense-to-period rule on the realized basis (FR-58); the FR-6 draft storage medium and its interaction with the Bangla input path; and the mechanism by which FR-61's "not merely emptied but unavailable" reports are gated. *Fix:* add these to §12 or resolve them inline; the first two are cheap to state and expensive to discover.
- **low** No screen inventory (§5 throughout) — surfaces are named in prose (Column Mapping step, variance disposition, batch review, completeness drill-down, variant matrix editor, five role-specific dashboards) but never enumerated, and FR-39's "absent, not masked" implies a per-Role navigation specification that does not exist. *Fix:* add a screen list with the Role that reaches each; it is the artifact the UX workflow will otherwise assemble by guesswork.

## Shape fit — adequate

Correct shape for a chain-top PRD feeding UX → architecture → stories: glossary first, journeys with a named protagonist, FRs with stable global IDs and inline UJ references, cross-cutting NFRs separated from feature requirements. Not over-formalized — one persona, six journeys, no stakeholder matrix theater. §11's reframing of metrics for an academic context with no users is the right call, stated as a reasoned decision rather than an excuse.

Where the shape strains: for a **solo 2–4 week** build the document is sized like a team PRD. 68 FRs, 21 NFRs, 9 SMs, 4 counter-metrics, 12 modules. The test obligations alone, counted literally from the requirement text — per-entity cross-tenant tests (FR-44), per-restricted-capability endpoint tests (FR-45), one reversal test per operation type (FR-64, SM-4), the movement-sum invariant (NFR-7), untranslated-string detection (FR-66), the Bangla conjunct fixture (FR-68), module-disabled core-flow tests (FR-65), plus SM-1's consistency assertion and SM-3's batch-close refusal — come to something like sixty tests before a single feature test. That is a defensible academic posture and it is not a three-week posture.

### Findings

- **high** Six requirements are over-engineered relative to a solo 2–4 week build, and none is on the retreat ladder — FR-46 (Platform Administrator: a second authentication context, its own authorisation model, its own list views, for a stakeholder who appears once in the document and is graded by nobody); FR-66 (full English locale doubling every string in a Bangla-first product whose §7 rationale is that the user thinks in Bangla); FR-67 (bidirectional Bengali numeral handling — display *and* input acceptance — which touches every numeric form field); FR-6 (local draft persistence with a restore prompt and a two-second write cadence); FR-40's image renderer (a second output target for the Bangla pipeline, distinct from PDF in canvas, layout, and font loading); FR-65's "why profit changed between two periods" (a variance-explanation engine sitting in the Thin tier). Together these are plausibly a week of the three or four available. *Fix:* move all six into §4.4 above rung 1; cut FR-46 and FR-67's input handling outright, and make FR-40's image a stretch on the text summary.
- **medium** FR-65 implements a capability two caps forbid (FR-65 bullet 1 vs §4.2 Reports, §4.1 Dashboard) — "why profit changed between two periods" is cross-period comparison, which the Reports row excludes ("cross-period comparison") and the Dashboard row excludes ("custom date-range comparison"). Worse, FR-65 bullet 2 requires each answer be "computed from the same figures the corresponding report would produce," and no report produces period-over-period profit change — so the Thin-tier module has to build the comparison engine the two higher tiers were forbidden from building, from scratch, with no source report. This is the clearest single case of the caps not surviving contact with the requirements. *Fix:* replace with an in-cap query ("profit for a period, with its basis and completeness") and note the deferral.
- **medium** Full-tier modules delivered at Thin-tier depth (FR-59, FR-60 vs §4.2) — Customer due management is one FR, and the cap's "due collection status" appears nowhere in FR-59. Supplier management is one FR whose "Supplier outstanding balance is recorded" uses passive voice to conceal that nothing creates it, nothing decrements it (§4.2 and FR-60 both exclude recording supplier payments), and the Supplier Due Report has no data source. A balance that can only increase is not a balance. Note also that **Supplier is not in the §3 glossary at all** despite being capitalised as a defined term in FR-49, FR-53, FR-60, and §4.2. *Fix:* define the Supplier balance as `sum of Stock-In cost against that Supplier − Supplier Payments recorded`, add a minimal Supplier Payment recording capability (which the cap currently excludes, so either amend the cap row or state that the balance is purchase-total-only and rename it), add a Supplier glossary entry, and add "due collection status" to FR-59.
- **low** Expense mutability unspecified (FR-57, FR-58) — FR-64 makes Expense entry reversible, which implies Expenses are not edited. No FR says so, and §8's "no destructive operation exists on financial or inventory history" does not name Expenses. *Fix:* one bullet in FR-57 stating that a recorded Expense is corrected by reversal per FR-64, not by editing.
- **low** Deletion rules applied inconsistently across master data (FR-19, FR-49 vs FR-47, FR-60) — Courier and Product both get an explicit "referenced ⇒ deactivate, not delete" rule. Customer and Supplier get none, though both are referenced by Orders and Stock-Ins respectively. *Fix:* state the same rule once as an NFR and drop the per-FR repetition.
